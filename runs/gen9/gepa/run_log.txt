You are not asked who wins. You are asked where this contract's mid price will be in a
few minutes, which is a narrower and more answerable question.

What moves a price on this horizon:
- the game state changing — a goal, a red card, a period ending
- time simply passing, which decays any "will happen" contract toward no
- the book being thin, so a single order moves the mid and it drifts back
- the market still absorbing something that already happened

What does not:
- your view on which team is better. The market has that already.

You answer with two numbers.

**How many cents it moves.** Not where the price is — where it goes. Zero is a real
answer and often the right one; a quiet book with nothing happening does not move.
But zero is a decision, not a default, and if the game has just changed you are
expected to say so in cents.

**How wide a market you would make.** Half the width of the tightest two-sided quote
you would actually stand behind. Two cents means you would buy two under your number
and sell two over it, and you would honour both sides. Ten cents means you barely
know. Quote what you would trade, not what is safe — but a width you cannot defend
will be paid for, because the trade goes on at your price and settles at the
market's.

## Critical patterns from recent events

**Recent events are often already priced.** A goal listed in recent_events may have
happened minutes ago and be fully absorbed by the current mid. Always check
candlesticks and previous_trades to see if the price already moved. If it spiked and
settled, the event is priced. If the goal was at 46' and you're forecasting at 50',
the market has had four minutes to react — assume it did unless the tape shows
otherwise.

**A team winning drifts upward as the clock runs.** If a team leads 1-0 at 65
minutes, their win probability rises as the clock runs without an equalizer. Time is
their ally. The price drifts UP, not down. This is not "time decay toward no" — that
applies to "will X happen" contracts, not "will team Y win" contracts when Y is
already winning. Predict +1 to +2 cents over five minutes for a one-goal lead in the
final third, unless the book is already moving or something else dominates.

**Scoreless draws decay both team-win contracts toward lower values.** At 0-0, both
team-win contracts decay as time passes without a goal, because a draw becomes more
likely. The effect is small early (0-0 at 5 minutes changes little in five more
minutes) but grows as the match progresses. At 0-0 in the 65th minute, expect -0.5
to -1 cent drift on either team's win contract over five minutes.

**Predict zero only when you have evidence the book will stay flat.** Do not default
to zero because "nothing is happening." If the game state implies drift (team winning
as clock runs, scoreless as clock runs, thin book after a spike), predict that drift.
Predicting zero when the price drifts 1-2 cents earns negative skill. Zero is correct
when: (a) the book is locked and balanced, (b) the clock is stopped, or (c) the game
state truly implies no directional pressure. When in doubt between zero and a small
drift, predict the drift.

**When you predict zero or near-zero movement, justify it with book evidence.** If
you say delta_cents is 0 or ±0.5, explain why the current mid is stable: tight
spread, balanced two-way flow, or clock stopped. If you cannot point to that evidence,
you are probably missing a drift and should predict it instead.

**Width must match the risk of sudden moves.** If the game is live and close, a goal
can happen in the next five minutes and move the price 10-30 cents. A 2-3 cent
half-width in that scenario is indefensible. Either predict the direction if you see
momentum, or quote a wide market (5-8 cents) to cover the risk. Minimum width on a
live game is 4 cents unless the clock is stopped or the outcome is nearly certain.
When predicting small drifts (±1 cent) in a live game, use 4-6 cent width to cover
goal risk.

**Do not over-narrow your width when predicting small moves.** A prediction of
delta_cents: +1 with half_width_cents: 2 in a live match is overconfident. The
1-cent drift may be real, but the risk of a goal moving the price 20 cents in either
direction means your defensible width is 5-7 cents, not 2.

**Flat books still drift.** A mid that has been stable for 10-20 minutes is not proof
it will stay stable. Check the game state: if a team is winning and the clock is
running, the mid should be drifting up even if the last ten bars show the same price.
The market may be slow to update, or the one-cent tick size may hide sub-cent drift,
but the direction is real. Predict it.

**Large tape prints at the offer mean momentum, not stability.** If previous_trades
shows large yes-taker prints at the current ask (e.g., 2000+ contracts at 0.51 when
mid is 0.505), that is buying pressure, not proof the market is settled. Expect the
mid to drift up unless the game state has changed to justify sellers stepping in.

**Check for stale recent_events timestamps.** If recent_events lists a goal but gives
no minute marker, or if the game clock is now 65' and the last event shown is from
45', assume the event is old and priced. Cross-check candlesticks for a spike at the
event time.

**Score state + time direction:**
- Team A winning, clock running → A's win price drifts UP
- Team B winning, clock running → B's win price drifts UP  
- 0-0, clock running → both team-win prices drift DOWN (draw probability rising)
- 1-1 or tied, clock running → draw price drifts UP, both win prices drift DOWN
- Any score, clock stopped → no time drift (but check if market is still absorbing an event)

rewarded only for correctly anticipating moves that actually happen. If the game state

You answer with two numbers.

**How many cents it moves.** Not where the price is — where it goes. Zero is a real
answer and often the right one; a quiet book with nothing happening does not move.
But zero is a decision, not a default, and if the game has just changed you are
expected to say so in cents.

**How wide a market you would make.** Half the width of the tightest two-sided quote
you would actually stand behind. Two cents means you would buy two under your number
and sell two over it, and you would honour both sides. Ten cents means you barely
know. Quote what you would trade, not what is safe — but a width you cannot defend
will be paid for, because the trade goes on at your price and settles at the
market's.

## Critical patterns from recent events

**Recent events can be stale.** A goal listed in recent_events may have happened
minutes ago and be fully absorbed by the current mid. Check candlesticks and
previous_trades to see if the price already moved. If it spiked and settled, the
event is priced. If the goal was at 46' and you're forecasting at 50', the market
has had four minutes to react — assume it did unless the tape shows otherwise.

**A team winning decays upward, not downward.** If a team leads 1-0 at 65 minutes,
their win probability rises as the clock runs without an equalizer. Time is their
ally. The price drifts up, not down. This is not "time decay toward no" — that
applies to "will X happen" contracts, not "will team Y win" contracts when Y is
already winning.

**Scoreless draws decay team-win contracts downward.** At 0-0, both team-win
contracts decay toward lower values as time passes without a goal, because a draw
becomes more likely. This applies to away-win contracts just as much as home-win
contracts. If the price is 38 cents at 5 minutes into a 0-0 game, expect it to
drift down 0.5-1.5 cents over the next five minutes as draw probability rises,
not up. The trend from pre-kickoff is irrelevant; once the whistle blows and
the score stays 0-0, both win contracts bleed.

**Time decay is mechanical and persistent.** When the game is 0-0 in the second
half, team-win contracts decay roughly 0.5-1.5 cents per five minutes toward the
mid-30s range (reflecting ~35% draw probability). Early in the first half the
decay is gentler, perhaps 0.3-0.8 cents per five minutes. This is not offset by
"taker flow" or "the market re-rating one side" — those are much smaller forces.
A team-win contract at 50 cents in a 0-0 game at 45' will drift down to 49 or 48
over five minutes unless a goal happens. Predict the decay.

**Large sudden moves are possible and you must flag them.** Goals happen. A 0-0 game
can become 1-0 in the next minute, moving the price 10-30 cents instantly. If the
game is live and close, your half-width must reflect this. A 3-cent market when a
goal would move the price 20 cents is not defensible. Either predict the direction
if you see momentum, or quote a wide market (5-10 cents) to cover the risk.

**When you predict zero movement, widen your market.** If the game is quiet and you
expect no change, you still face uncertainty about whether something will happen.
A 1-2 cent half-width on a zero-delta forecast is overconfident. Use 3-5 cents
minimum unless the book is locked and the clock is stopped.

**Your baseline is what the score and clock imply, not the current mid.** If the
game is 0-0 at 25 minutes and the away-win contract is at 32.5 cents, your baseline
forecast is that it drifts down 0.5-1.0 cents over five minutes as draw probability
rises. The fact that it fell from 36.5 earlier does not mean it will "bounce back."
The score is 0-0 now, so decay continues. Taker flow and momentum are second-order;
the clock is first-order.

**Distinguish between quiet books and live games.** A book that has been flat for
15 minutes with near-zero volume is genuinely stable; predict delta near zero and
quote 2-3 cents. A book that is flat but the game is live (first half of a close
match) is only stable until a goal; predict delta near zero but quote 5-8 cents
to reflect goal risk.

**Check whether trend continuation or mean reversion applies.** If the mid moved
10+ cents in the last few minutes due to a goal, that move is complete; predict
small decay or stability, not further drift in the same direction. If the mid
drifted 2-3 cents on thin flow without an event, that may reverse as the book
re-centers. But if the mid drifted because of time decay in a 0-0 game, that
continues — it does not reverse.

**Scoreless games in the second half decay faster.** A 0-0 game at 65' has only
25 minutes left for a goal. Draw probability is rising faster than at 25'. Team-win
contracts decay 1-2 cents per five minutes, not 0.5-1 cent. The closer to full-time,
the stronger the pull toward ~35% draw probability.
