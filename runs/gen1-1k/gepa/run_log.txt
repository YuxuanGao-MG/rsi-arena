**Time decay for a team WINNING goes UP, not down.** This is the most common error.

an equalizer. Each passing minute makes it harder for the opponent to score. The
price drifts UP. This is not "time decay toward no" — that applies to "will X happen"
contracts. A team defending a lead grows MORE likely to win as time expires.

**Time decay for a team LOSING goes DOWN.** If Chelsea trails 0-1 at 70', their win
probability FALLS as time passes. Fewer minutes remain to equalize and then win.
The price drifts DOWN.

**Scoreless draws decay both ways toward a coin flip.** At 0-0, both team-win
contracts decay toward lower values as time passes without a goal, because a draw
becomes more likely. But this is a weak effect early in the match.

**Absorption takes time but not as much as you think.** A goal moves the price
10-30 cents instantly, but full absorption happens within 2-3 minutes, not 10-15.
If a goal happened 6+ minutes ago and candlesticks show the price has been stable
for 3+ minutes, it is fully priced. Do not predict continued "absorption drift"
long after the event. The market is faster than you expect.

**After absorption, time decay dominates.** Once a goal is absorbed and the price
stabilizes, the next driver is time decay. If the score is not level, the winning
team's price drifts up (or the losing team's price drifts down) at roughly 0.5-1.5
cents per five minutes in the first half, and faster (1-3 cents per five minutes)
in the second half when time remaining matters more.

**When you predict zero movement, widen your market significantly.** If the game is
quiet and you expect no change, you still face uncertainty about whether something
will happen. A 2-3 cent half-width on a zero-delta forecast is overconfident when
the game is live. Use 3-5 cents for a quiet game in the first half, 5-8 cents in
the second half when goals are more likely and move the price further.

**Large sudden moves are possible and you must account for them in width.** Goals
happen. A 0-0 game can become 1-0 in the next minute, moving the price 15-25 cents
instantly. If the game is live and close, your half-width must reflect this. A 3-cent
market when a goal would move the price 20 cents is not defensible. Either predict
the direction if you see signs of momentum, or quote a wide market (5-10 cents) to
cover the two-sided risk.

**The current mid is not your baseline.** Predicting delta_cents: 0 because the mid
is currently stable earns exactly zero skill. You are rewarded only for correctly
anticipating moves that actually happen. If the game state suggests the price should
drift up 1-2 cents over five minutes due to time decay, say so. But if you are
unsure of the direction, zero is better than guessing wrong.

**Check the score and clock to determine time decay direction.** Before predicting:
1. Is one team winning? The winning team's price drifts UP as time passes.
2. Is the score level? Both team prices drift DOWN slightly (draw becomes more likely).
3. How much time remains? More time remaining = slower decay. Less time = faster decay.
4. Has a recent goal been absorbed? Check candlesticks. If stable for 3+ minutes, it's priced.

**When trailing by a goal late in the match, decay accelerates downward.** A team
losing 0-1 at 80' has their price collapse faster than at 60'. The effect is
nonlinear. Do not underestimate late-game time decay.
