You are not asked who wins. You are asked where this contract's mid price will be in a
few minutes: delta_cents (how many cents it moves) and half_width_cents (half the

## What moves prices on this horizon

**Goals and cards the market hasn't absorbed yet.** A goal 2-3 minutes ago may still be
moving the price as the book digests it. Look at candlestick patterns: a spike followed
by drift means absorption is ongoing.

**Time decay.** When a team is behind and the clock runs, their win probability decays
toward zero. The rate accelerates as time runs out: -1c per 5 minutes with 30 minutes
left becomes -2 to -4c per 5 minutes with 15 minutes left. A draw contract decays
toward certainty (100) when the leading team protects their lead late.

**Thin books drifting.** A single large order moves the mid, then it drifts back over
minutes. Recent trades show if this is happening: one-sided flow at a new level that
doesn't hold.

**Score changes.** Not just that a goal happened, but whether the price has fully
adjusted. A trailing team's contract drops when they concede, but may continue drifting
down for several minutes after.

## What does not move prices

Your view on team quality. The market prices that already. A yellow card in minute 15
when the score is 0-0. Substitutions unless they coincide with other events.

## Critical facts about these contracts

**Ticker format:** KXEPLGAME-[DATE][HOMECODE][AWAYCODE]-[WINNING_TEAM_CODE]

The last segment tells you which team the contract pays on. KXEPLGAME-26SEP06EVEMUN-MUN
is a contract on Manchester United (MUN) winning. If United leads 1-0, this contract is
in the money and rises with time decay. If Everton leads 1-0, this contract decays
toward zero.

**Always check:** Does the score favor the contract team or oppose it? A 0-1 scoreline
where away is winning means very different things for a home-team contract versus an
away-team contract.

## Time decay: the dominant driver and most common error

**Time decay is NOT always negligible.** The feedback shows harnesses constantly
underestimate decay magnitude. With 25 minutes left and trailing by one goal, decay
is real and measurable over five minutes, not ignorable noise.

**Time decay is NOT linear.** It accelerates as time runs out:
- 40+ minutes left: ~0-0.5c per 5 minutes (often undetectable)
- 25-35 minutes left: ~0.5-1.5c per 5 minutes (real but gradual)
- 15-25 minutes left: ~1-3c per 5 minutes (the main event)
- Under 15 minutes: ~2-5c per 5 minutes (rapid collapse)

**But:** Example 8 shows that even at 65' (25 min left), PHI trailing 0-1, predicted
-2c decay, the price was **flat** over five minutes. Why? Because five minutes is short
relative to 25 minutes remaining. The decay that happens over five minutes when 25
minutes remain is small enough to be swamped by bid-ask noise, order flow, and the
discrete nature of tick sizes.

**The key insight:** Time decay over a five-minute window is only detectable when
urgency is high (under 20 minutes) OR when the book is thin/stable enough that sub-cent
forces can move the mid. At 65' with 25 minutes left, even a trailing team's decay
might be only 0.3-0.8c over five minutes—easily masked by a 1-cent spread and
normal flow.

**Revised heuristic for time decay:**
- Over 30 minutes left: predict zero decay unless the book is drifting already
- 20-30 minutes left: predict 0-1c decay for trailing teams, only if book is quiet
- 15-20 minutes left: predict 1-2c decay for trailing teams
- 10-15 minutes left: predict 2-4c decay for trailing teams
- Under 10 minutes: predict 3-6c decay for trailing teams

**Critical:** If the book has been flat for 10+ bars and you're outside the final
20 minutes, predict zero even if theory says decay exists. The five-minute window
is too short to reliably see it.

## When to predict zero (and when NOT to)

**Predict zero when:**
- Score is level and more than 25 minutes remain (no decay driver, no recent shock)
- Book has been flat for 10+ minutes with balanced two-way flow
- Early in the match (first 20 minutes, 0-0) with a stable book
- The contract team is trailing but more than 30 minutes remain and book is stable

**DO NOT predict zero when:**
- A goal happened in the last 5-10 minutes and candlesticks show ongoing drift
- The contract team is trailing with under 20 minutes left (decay is real)
- Recent trades show one-sided flow at a new level (ongoing absorption or drift-back)
- The book just moved and hasn't stabilized (multiple bars at different levels)

**Common error from feedback:** Predicting zero "because the book looks stable" when
actually the book has been DRIFTING slowly over 10-20 bars. Stable means the mid is
unchanged for 5+ bars, not that it moved gradually and stopped one minute ago.

## Thin books and quote artifacts

**Example 4 teaches a critical lesson:** The exchange mid was 28c but the last trade
was 32c and all recent trades were at 31-32c. The harness correctly predicted +2.5c
drift back toward the trade price (realized +3.5c).

**When trade price and mid diverge:**
- Recent trades at 32c, mid at 28c with 6-cent spread: the mid is a quote artifact
- Predict drift toward the trade price, not the mid (but remember: delta_cents applies
  to the exchange mid, so you predict the mid moving toward where trades are)
- Wide spreads (4+ cents) on low volume often mean the mid is unreliable

**How to spot a thin book:**
- Spread above 3 cents
- Candlestick volume near zero for multiple bars
- Last trade price far from mid
- When these align, the mid is a phantom—predict where it will drift to match reality

## Early-match stability vs. late-match urgency

**First 30 minutes, 0-0 or close score:** Even with volume and flow, price moves are
often mean-reverting noise. Example 3 and Example 7 show early-game predictions of
zero being correct even when there's moderate trading activity. Unless there's a
clear post-goal drift or one-sided flow that persists across multiple bars, predict
zero early.

**Final 20 minutes, any score gap:** Price moves become directional. Decay dominates
for trailing teams, and even leading teams' "lock it in" premium grows. The same
1-goal gap that does nothing at 60' can drive 2-3c over five minutes at 75'.

## The "flat for how long?" test

Before predicting zero, count how many consecutive bars the mid has been at the
current level:
- 1-2 bars: too soon, might still be drifting
- 3-5 bars: probably stable if flow is balanced
- 6+ bars: definitely stable, predict zero unless time decay is urgent (under 20 min)

Example 1: 17 flat bars at 60.5c after halftime, balanced flow → correctly predicted zero.

Example 8: 12 bars at ~50.5c, but still 25 min left → predicted -2c, realized 0c.
The book WAS flat and decay over five minutes was undetectable.

## Absorption after a goal

A goal causes a spike (5-15c jump), then the price drifts over 5-15 minutes as the
book absorbs the new information. 

**How to tell if absorption is ongoing:**
- Spike visible in candlesticks 3-10 minutes ago
- Mid has been drifting (changing by 0.5-1c every few bars) since
- Trade flow is one-sided (takers keep lifting the same side)

**How to tell if absorption is complete:**
- Mid has been flat for 5+ bars
- Trade flow is balanced (yes and no takers alternating)
- Volume has returned to normal levels

Example 1: Goal at ~45', halftime break, then 17 flat bars at 60.5c → absorption
complete, correctly predicted zero.

## Checking your own prediction

Before you finalize:

1. **Did I check the ticker?** Which team does this contract pay on?
2. **Does the score favor or oppose that team?** Home 1 - Away 0, ticker ends in
   away-team code → contract is LOSING.
3. **How much time is left?** Under 20 minutes = decay matters. Over 30 = probably not.
4. **Has the mid been flat for 5+ bars?** If yes and no urgency, predict zero.
5. **Is there a wide spread with trades far from mid?** If yes, predict drift toward
   trade price.
6. **Did a goal happen in the last 10 minutes?** Check if drift is ongoing (changing
   mid across bars) or complete (flat for 5+ bars).
7. **Am I predicting decay in the right direction?** Trailing team → negative.
   Leading team or draw when leading → positive. Level score → zero.

## Your two numbers

**Delta_cents: where it goes, not where it is.** Zero is real and often right when
nothing is happening. But if the game state is actively driving the price (time decay
with under 20 minutes left, post-goal drift still ongoing), you must call it. The
error in Example 8 was predicting -2c when the book had been flat for 12 bars with
25 minutes left—the five-minute window was too short to see that decay.

**Half_width_cents: what you'd actually trade.** Two cents means you buy at your
prediction minus 2 and sell at plus 2, and you honor both sides. If you're unsure,
widen the quote, but remember: fees are 2-3 cents, so a 10-cent-wide market is too
wide to be useful. Quote 1.5-3 cents when you're confident, 3-5 cents when uncertain,
wider only when genuinely lost.

2. **Predicting zero when drift is ongoing.** If the mid has changed over the last
   3-5 bars, it's probably still moving. Don't call it stable prematurely.

3. **Ignoring the trade tape when the mid is wide.** Example 4: trades at 32c, mid

4. **Overestimating decay magnitude.** Even under 20 minutes, decay over five minutes

5. **Forgetting to check ticker vs. score.** If the contract is on the trailing team,
   decay is negative. If on the leading team, decay is positive. Get this wrong and
   you predict the opposite of reality.

6. **Predicting zero "by default."** Zero is not a fallback; it's a specific prediction
   that the mid will be unchanged. Only predict it when you have evidence: flat book,
   balanced flow, no urgency, no ongoing drift. Otherwise, call the direction even if

## What moves prices on this horizon

**Goals and cards the market hasn't absorbed yet.** A goal 2-3 minutes ago may still be
moving the price as the book digests it. Look at candlestick patterns: a spike followed
by drift means absorption is ongoing.

**Time decay.** When a team is behind and the clock runs, their win probability decays
toward zero. The rate accelerates as time runs out: -1c per 5 minutes with 30 minutes
left becomes -2 to -4c per 5 minutes with 15 minutes left. A draw contract decays
toward certainty (100) when the leading team protects their lead late.

**Thin books drifting.** A single large order moves the mid, then it drifts back over
minutes. Recent trades show if this is happening: one-sided flow at a new level that
doesn't hold.

**Score changes.** Not just that a goal happened, but whether the price has fully
adjusted. A trailing team's contract drops when they concede, but may continue drifting
down for several minutes after.

## What does not move prices

Your view on team quality. The market prices that already. A yellow card in minute 15
when the score is 0-0. Substitutions unless they coincide with other events.

## Critical facts about these contracts

**Ticker format:** KXEPLGAME-[DATE][HOMECODE][AWAYCODE]-[WINNING_TEAM_CODE]

The last segment tells you which team the contract pays on. KXEPLGAME-26SEP06EVEMUN-MUN
is a contract on Manchester United (MUN) winning. If United leads 1-0, this contract is
in the money and rises with time decay. If Everton leads 1-0, this contract decays
toward zero.

**Always check:** Does the score favor the contract team or oppose it? A 0-1 scoreline
where away is winning means very different things for a home-team contract versus an
away-team contract.

## Time decay: massively overestimated in the examples

**The central lesson from feedback:** The harness predicted decay in Examples 3, 7, and 8,

**Five minutes is a very short window.** Even when time decay exists in theory, it may

floor of bid-ask bounce, order flow randomness, and tick discretization.

**When decay is actually invisible over five minutes:**
- More than 20 minutes remain in the match
- The contract is already at a low price (under 15 cents)
- The book has been flat for 5+ bars

**Revised heuristic for time decay:**
- **Over 25 minutes left:** Predict zero unless the book is actively drifting already.
  Five minutes is too short to see decay.
- **20-25 minutes left:** Predict zero unless the book shows clear directional movement.
  Decay may exist but is likely under 0.5c over five minutes.
- **15-20 minutes left:** Predict 0.5-1.5c decay only if the book is thin or you see
  gradual drift. If flat for 5+ bars, predict zero.
- **10-15 minutes left:** Predict 1-2c decay for trailing teams if the book is moving.
  If flat, predict 0.5-1c at most.
- **Under 10 minutes:** Predict 2-4c decay for trailing teams. This is when urgency
  finally dominates noise.

**The "flat book" rule:** If the mid has been at the same level (±0.5c) for 5+ consecutive
