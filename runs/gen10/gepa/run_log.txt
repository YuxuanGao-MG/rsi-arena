## What moves prices on this horizon

**Goals and cards the market hasn't absorbed yet.** A goal 2-3 minutes ago may still be
moving the price as the book digests it. Look at candlestick patterns: a spike followed
by drift means absorption is ongoing.

**Time decay.** When a team is behind and the clock runs, their win probability decays
toward zero. The rate accelerates as time runs out: -1c per 5 minutes with 30 minutes
left becomes -2 to -4c per 5 minutes with 15 minutes left. A draw contract decays
toward certainty (100) when the leading team protects their lead late.

**Thin books drifting.** A single large order moves the mid, then it drifts back over
minutes. Recent trades show if this is happening: one-sided flow at a new level that
doesn't hold.

**Score changes.** Not just that a goal happened, but whether the price has fully
adjusted. A trailing team's contract drops when they concede, but may continue drifting
down for several minutes after.

## What does not move prices

Your view on team quality. The market prices that already. A yellow card in minute 15
when the score is 0-0. Substitutions unless they coincide with other events.

## Critical facts about these contracts

**Ticker format:** KXEPLGAME-[DATE][HOMECODE][AWAYCODE]-[WINNING_TEAM_CODE]

The last segment tells you which team the contract pays on. KXEPLGAME-26SEP06EVEMUN-MUN
is a contract on Manchester United (MUN) winning. If United leads 1-0, this contract is
in the money and rises with time decay. If Everton leads 1-0, this contract decays
toward zero.

**Always check:** Does the score favor the contract team or oppose it? A 0-1 scoreline
where away is winning means very different things for a home-team contract versus an
away-team contract.

## Time decay: the most common driver

**When to expect decay:**
- Trailing team contracts decay toward zero as time runs out
- Leading team contracts rise toward 100 as time runs out
- Draw contracts rise when the score is level and both teams play conservatively late

**Rates matter but vary more than you think:**
- With 40+ minutes left and one goal behind: 0.5-1c per 5 minutes
- With 25-30 minutes left and one goal behind: 1-2c per 5 minutes  
- With 15-20 minutes left and one goal behind: 2-4c per 5 minutes
- With <10 minutes left and one goal behind: 4-8c per 5 minutes

**But decay is not universal.** At 0-0 in minute 5, or 1-1 at halftime, there is no
decay driver. Both sides have time. The score is level. Predict zero unless other
factors dominate.

## When to predict zero vs. when to call a move

**Predict zero when:**
- Score is level and plenty of time remains (e.g., 0-0 at 5', 1-1 at halftime)
- Book is stable with balanced two-way flow and no recent shocks
- No clear time-decay or post-goal drift driver exists

**Call a move when:**
- Team is trailing with <25 minutes left: predict decay in the correct direction
- Candlesticks show post-goal drift still happening (spike + continued drift)
- Thin book with one-sided recent flow reverting toward fair value
- Draw contract at 2-2 with <20 minutes left: rises toward 100 as clock runs

**The error pattern:** The harness often predicts zero when decay should be obvious
(trailing at 65') or calls decay when the game is level with time (0-0 at 5', 1-1 at
45'). Match the prediction to the actual situation.

## Post-goal absorption

Goals cause immediate spikes, but absorption takes time. If candlesticks show:
- A goal 10+ minutes ago
- Price spiked immediately, then stabilized
- Multiple flat bars at the new level with balanced flow

→ Absorption is complete. Predict zero unless decay takes over.

If candlesticks show:
- A goal 2-5 minutes ago
- Price spiked, then continued drifting in one direction
- One-sided flow or erratic mid movement

→ Absorption ongoing. Predict continuation of the drift (usually 1-3c over 5 minutes).

## The goal ambiguity problem

Sometimes the game feed and the price violently disagree. Example: feed shows Roma
trailing 0-1 (implying ~20c price) but the contract trades at 58c with heavy buying.

**Trust the price.** The feed may be delayed, wrong, or you may have misread the ticker.
A contract trading at 58c with in-the-money flow behavior is almost certainly in the
money. If the stated score makes no sense with the price, assume the score is stale and
use the price direction + flow as your signal.

## Your two numbers

**Delta_cents: where it goes, not where it is.** Zero is real and often right when
nothing is happening (level score, early game, stable book). But if the game state is
actively driving the price (time decay with <25 minutes left and trailing, post-goal
drift continuing), you must call it. Magnitude:
- 0c: level score, no driver, stable book
- ±1-2c: moderate decay or slow post-goal drift
- ±2-4c: strong decay (<20 min left, one goal behind)
- ±5c+: near-floor/ceiling, extreme time pressure, or ongoing shock absorption

**Half_width_cents: what you'd actually trade.** Two cents means you buy at your
prediction minus 2 and sell at plus 2, and you honor both sides. If you're unsure,
widen the quote, but remember: fees are 2-3 cents, so a 10-cent-wide market is too
wide to be useful. Quote 1.5-3 cents when you're confident, 3-5 cents when uncertain,
wider only when genuinely lost.

## Common errors to avoid

**Don't predict decay when the score is level early.** 0-0 at 5', 1-1 at 45' with a
full half to play: neither side is decaying. Predict zero unless other factors dominate.

**Don't predict zero when decay is obvious.** Trailing by one goal at 65' with 25

**Don't assume absorption always takes time.** A goal 15+ minutes ago that caused an

zero unless decay takes over.

**Don't ignore the direction of decay.** If the contract team is winning, their contract
rises with time. If losing, it falls. Check the ticker suffix and the score carefully.
