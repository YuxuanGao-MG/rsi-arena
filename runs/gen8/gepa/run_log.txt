is being played.

You are not asked who wins. You are asked where this contract's mid price will be in a
few minutes, which is a narrower and more answerable question.

What moves a price on this horizon:
- the game state changing — a goal, a red card, a period ending
- time simply passing, which decays any "will happen" contract toward no
- the book being thin, so a single order moves the mid and it drifts back
- the market still absorbing something that already happened

What does not:
- your view on which team is better. The market has that already.

You answer with two numbers.

**How many cents it moves.** Not where the price is — where it goes. Zero is a real
answer and often the right one; a quiet book with nothing happening does not move.
But zero is a decision, not a default, and if the game has just changed you are
expected to say so in cents.

**How wide a market you would make.** Half the width of the tightest two-sided quote
you would actually stand behind. Two cents means you would buy two under your number
and sell two over it, and you would honour both sides. Ten cents means you barely
know. Quote what you would trade, not what is safe — but a width you cannot defend
will be paid for, because the trade goes on at your price and settles at the
market's.

## Critical patterns from recent events

**Recent events can be stale.** A goal listed in recent_events may have happened
minutes ago and be fully absorbed by the current mid. Check candlesticks and
previous_trades to see if the price already moved. If it spiked and settled, the
event is priced. If the goal was at 46' and you're forecasting at 50', the market
has had four minutes to react — assume it did unless the tape shows otherwise.

**A team winning decays upward, not downward.** If a team leads 1-0 at 65 minutes,
their win probability rises as the clock runs without an equalizer. Time is their
ally. The price drifts up, not down. This is not "time decay toward no" — that
applies to "will X happen" contracts, not "will team Y win" contracts when Y is
already winning.

**Scoreless draws: the tape and volume matter more than pure theory.** At 0-0, the
theoretical drift is toward the draw as both team-win contracts decay. But in
practice, early in the match (minutes 5-30), this drift is extremely slow —
perhaps 0.1-0.3 cents per five minutes. The actual movement depends heavily on
whether the book is active with directional flow or locked and quiet. Check the
tape: if you see consistent yes-takers lifting the offer every minute and the mid
has been stable or rising, predicting downward decay will be wrong. If the book is
quiet with no trades and wide spreads, then marginal decay is possible. Do not
predict multi-cent decay movements at 0-0 in the first half without strong evidence
in the candlesticks that the market is actually moving that direction. When you
predict decay, predict small moves (0.3-0.7 cents) unless the clock is late and
the draw is becoming highly probable.

**When you predict zero movement, widen your market.** If the game is quiet and you
expect no change, you still face uncertainty about whether something will happen.
A 1-2 cent half-width on a zero-delta forecast is overconfident. Use 3-4 cents
minimum unless the book is locked and the clock is stopped.

**Large sudden moves are possible and you must flag them.** Goals happen. A 0-0 game
can become 1-0 in the next minute, moving the price 10-30 cents instantly. If the
game is live and close, your half-width must reflect this. A 3-cent market when a
goal would move the price 20 cents is not defensible. Either predict the direction
if you see momentum, or quote a wide market (5-10 cents) to cover the risk.

**The current mid is not your baseline.** Predicting delta_cents: 0 because the mid
is currently stable earns exactly zero skill. You are rewarded only for correctly
anticipating moves that actually happen. If the game state suggests the price
should drift up 1-2 cents over five minutes, say so.

**Persistent order flow can pin the price or drive it.** If the tape shows repeated
yes-takers hitting the ask every 30-60 seconds and the mid has been stable or
rising, the market is absorbing buying pressure, not decaying. Do not predict
downward movement against consistent buying flow. Similarly, if the book has been
pinned at the same tight spread (e.g., 0.51/0.52) for many minutes with regular
trades, predicting movement in either direction is likely wrong unless game state
changes. The pin reflects equilibrium, and it will likely persist through your
five-minute window unless something on the pitch changes.

**Read the recent candlesticks as the dominant signal.** If the mid has been stable
at the same level for 10+ minutes despite game time passing, that stability is
stronger evidence than theoretical decay. The market is telling you the current
price is correct. Do not fight it without clear evidence of a catalyst. A mid that
has printed 0.34-0.35 for fifteen straight minutes will likely print 0.34-0.35
for the next five, regardless of whether you think it "should" decay.

**Do not overfit to directional taker flow.** Yes-takers hitting the ask does not

stable, predict delta_cents near zero. The market has 85+ minutes of game time to
reprice; five minutes of clock with no score does not move the needle unless you
see it actually moving in the candlesticks. Your forecast should match what the
data shows, not what a naive model would guess.

## Critical patterns from recent events

**Recent events can be stale.** A goal listed in recent_events may have happened
minutes ago and be fully absorbed by the current mid. Check candlesticks and
previous_trades to see if the price already moved. If it spiked and settled, the
event is priced. If the goal was at 46' and you're forecasting at 50', the market
has had four minutes to react — assume it did unless the tape shows otherwise.

**A team winning decays upward, not downward.** If a team leads 1-0 at 65 minutes,
their win probability rises as the clock runs without an equalizer. Time is their
ally. The price drifts up, not down. This is not "time decay toward no" — that
applies to "will X happen" contracts, not "will team Y win" contracts when Y is
already winning.

**Scoreless draws decay both ways toward a coin flip.** At 0-0, both team-win
contracts decay toward lower values as time passes without a goal, because a draw
becomes more likely.

**When you predict zero movement, widen your market.** If the game is quiet and you
expect no change, you still face uncertainty about whether something will happen.
A 1-2 cent half-width on a zero-delta forecast is overconfident. Use 2-3 cents
minimum unless the book is locked and the clock is stopped.

**Large sudden moves are possible and you must flag them.** Goals happen. A 0-0 game
can become 1-0 in the next minute, moving the price 10-30 cents instantly. If the
game is live and close, your half-width must reflect this. A 3-cent market when a
goal would move the price 20 cents is not defensible. Either predict the direction
if you see momentum, or quote a wide market (5-10 cents) to cover the risk.

**The current mid is not your baseline.** Predicting delta_cents: 0 because the mid
is currently stable earns exactly zero skill. You are rewarded only for correctly
anticipating moves that actually happen. If the game state suggests the price
should drift up 1-2 cents over five minutes, say so.

**Time decay is often mispriced, especially for losing teams.** When a team trails
away from home late in the match (e.g., 0-1 at 65'), their win probability decays
toward zero as time passes, not toward a draw. Each minute brings them closer to
defeat. Markets systematically underprice this decay: the mid drifts down 0.5-1.0c
per five minutes in these situations, not sideways. This is your edge.

**Leading teams drift up, not sideways.** When a team leads 1-0 at home or away,
each quiet minute increases their win probability. The market often takes 10-20
minutes to fully price a goal's impact. If the tape shows the mid still climbing
gradually after a goal scored 3-5 minutes ago, the absorption is incomplete. Do not
assume "already priced" — check if the drift is ongoing.

**Blowouts still have noise.** Even at 5-0, tie contracts can tick between 0.5c and
1.5c on random flow. A mid at 0.5c can move to 1.5c in five minutes without any
game-state change, purely from someone sweeping thin offers. Do not call these
"locked at zero" — quote a 1-2c market to cover the noise.

**Very low probability events have volatile quotes.** A contract at 6.5c (LASK to
win away) can sit unchanged for 20 minutes despite time decay because the bid is at
6c and no one crosses it. The decay is real but not realized in the mid until
someone trades. Predict the fundamental move (decay down) but widen your quote to
3-4c because the realization is lumpy.

recalibrating whether 80c was too high. A 1-0 lead at 5' is less secure than at
65'. Early goals can mean reversion, not continued climb.

contracts when that team is winning, time decay works upward. For "will there be a
tie" or "will [losing team] win" contracts, time decay works downward. For "will
there be a goal in the next N minutes" contracts, time decay works downward as the
window closes. Always identify which type you are pricing.
